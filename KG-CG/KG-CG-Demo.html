What this shows

Knowledge graph — the durable model: the agency account, its priority plan and the SLA written against it, the slice, data network name and sessions, cells, both SMFs and the UPF, the Nchf routes and CHF pools, then mediation, the CDR store, rating and billing. Plus the change records that touched them. It persists across every incident.

Two different time spans appear in the scenarios and it is worth keeping them apart. The measurement window is the few minutes of counter data the incident is scoped to. The change lookback is the seven days of change records searched for a plausible trigger, so a change dated a day or two before the incident can still be a candidate.

Context graph — what the system knows about this incident: observations, evidence pointing back at knowledge-graph entities, hypotheses and proposed actions, each with a source and a confidence. Created when the anchor fires, discarded on Reset.

Driving the demo

Pick a scenario from the blue selector in the header, or step through them with ‹ and ›. Then Play to walk the stages, or Step to advance while you narrate. Speed sets the dwell time. Arrow keys step, space plays and pauses.

Sizing it for your display

Drag the vertical handle between the topology and the detail panels to rebalance them. Double-click the handle to reset it, or focus it and use the arrow keys. The detail panels reflow into two columns once you give them the room.

Zoom scales the diagram in real pixels, so the labels never shrink below a readable size — the pane scrolls instead. Fit width fills the pane once, and stops tracking after that. Fit all shows the whole diagram with no scrolling, which makes it smaller, and keeps tracking as you drag the handle or resize the window. + and − zoom. Expand hides the detail panels entirely.

Un-reached stages in the narrowing rail read “not yet revealed” rather than sitting empty. The right-hand panels appear as the case file earns them: the live overlay, then the ranked candidates, then the action gate.

Reading the graph

Blue is the current candidate set. Faded entities were ruled out by an earlier stage but stay on screen so the narrowing is visible; entities that never entered scope at all are fainter still. Violet marks a located cause, and covers more than one entity where the cause genuinely spans several — a firmware build across three sectors, for instance. It appears only once the candidates have been ranked, so the diagram never gives the answer away early, and in the scenarios that never establish a cause nothing turns violet at all. Amber dashed boxes along the bottom are change records, linked to the entities they targeted.

Scores

Candidate scores are evidence-match scores against SME-authored failure fingerprints, not probabilities. A candidate is action-eligible only above threshold and only after the guardrail checks pass. A candidate marked unscorable and shown with a dash carries no number at all, because the measurement it would be scored against is itself unavailable — scoring it zero would claim an elimination the data cannot support. Three actions are permanently blocked from automation in every scenario: customer credit posting, direct billing-state writes, and RAN firmware rollback on cells serving an active public-safety response.

Ask the case file

Three pre-written questions per scenario, answered from the case file. Every figure quoted in an answer also appears in the panels above it, so the two can be checked against each other, and each answer names the source it was drawn from.

Tick “answer the same question with the graph evidence withheld” to see the same question answered from the incident description alone. Those answers are vague where the grounded one is specific, and in several scenarios they are confidently wrong — which is the comparison the panel exists to make.

Free-text questions are coming soon, with a selectable provider and model. Nothing else on the page depends on them: every ranking, control comparison and gate decision on screen is deterministic.

What changed in V2.3 (August 2026)

Three entities were named in the panels but never drawn. The capacity scenario cited a UPF in its signal table and an alternate SMF in its control comparison, and the entitlement-drift scenario scoped the incident to a slice; none of the three appeared at any stage, so you were asked to weigh evidence about elements that were not on the graph. All three now appear at the stage where they are first named.

What changed in V2.2 (August 2026)

A second accuracy pass, this time over the reasoning rather than the wording. The capacity scenario no longer approves a failover along a route it also calls unconfigured, and no longer holds a trigger-side candidate open on a signature it does not share: charging events that are never generated show a fall in Nchf request volume and no errors, which is the opposite of what that scenario measures. The trigger-side scenario stops claiming in one card that route-side causes are excluded and in the next that they cannot be separated — the request count is taken at the sender, so a shortfall there is requests never sent.

Four scenarios reported zero at the final stage while naming concrete open items in the same panel; they now count them. The stage-one failure catalogue is one number across every scenario, because a catalogue does not change with the incident. The queue counter is measured in seconds, so it is named queue wait rather than queue depth. Duplicate rate and reported completeness now reconcile, the incident window covers the 15-minute credit trigger it quotes, and the S-NSSAI is split into the SST and SD it is actually made of.

Four interaction defects went with them: an arrow key on the focused split handle both resized the pane and stepped the run; the space bar could not activate a focused rail segment; the evidence-withheld toggle survived a scenario change; and the narrowing rail forced seven columns on a phone, overriding its own responsive rule. The anchor line no longer shows the incident figures while its header still reads “not yet fired”.

What changed in V2.1 (August 2026)

A word-by-word accuracy pass over every scenario. The firmware scenario now shows the small, lagging Nchf elevation it always described rather than reporting it as baseline, and the candidate that dismissed charging capacity on the grounds of “no errors” now dismisses it on the grounds that matter: the elevation is 2× rather than 200×, queueing is flat, and the onset trails the session drops by 90 seconds.

A located cause may now cover more than one entity, so a firmware regression across three sectors marks all three violet instead of arbitrarily picking one. In the identifier-mismatch scenario the candidate that cannot be scored is marked unscorable and shown with a dash, because scoring it zero would claim an elimination the broken join cannot support. The measurement window and the seven-day change lookback are now named separately wherever both appear.

What changed in V2.0 (August 2026)

Pre-written questions and answers went live in Ask the case file, including the evidence-withheld comparison, with each question locked until the run reaches the stage that can answer it — the first two at stage 5 with the ranked candidates, the third at stage 6 with the action gate.

The panels were given a visible hierarchy: entity names picked out in colour, figures weighted against the prose, abnormal signal values sized and coloured by state while baseline rows recede, and control groups tagged pass or missing. Controls were restyled for legibility, un-reached stages in the narrowing rail say so rather than sitting empty, and a back-to-top button appears bottom-right once you scroll.

Renaming entities

Settings → Rename entities replaces any entity label with your own, everywhere it appears including the evidence prose. Stored in this browser only.

Scope

Illustrative and vendor-neutral. The operator is generic: PLMN 001-01 is the 3GPP test network, the market code and every element name are invented, and vendors are deliberately unnamed. All identifiers, counters, agency names, thresholds, timestamps and change records are fictional and represent no real network, product, customer or operational data. Charging-plane roles and interfaces follow 3GPP TS 32.240, 32.255, 32.290 and 32.291.
