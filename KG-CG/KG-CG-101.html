What this course is

A self-paced introduction to knowledge graphs and context graphs: what each one is, what makes them different, how they bind together, and how that pairing produces an answer you can defend rather than a guess you have to trust.

How to move around

  • The bar under the header lists all ten sections — the eight teaching modules, then the glossary and the references. Click any one to jump straight to it; nothing is locked.
  • Previous and Next at the bottom of each module walk the intended order.
  • The home icon returns to the course overview and your progress.

Keyboard

  • ← and → move between modules.
  • Esc closes this dialog.

Progress and quizzes

  • Each of the eight teaching modules ends with five questions. Every answer explains itself, including the wrong ones. There is one attempt per question — reloading the module gives you a fresh set.
  • Four correct out of five marks a module complete, so the progress bar counts those eight. Progress is saved in this browser only — no account, no server, nothing leaves your machine.
  • Use Reset course progress below to clear it.

Interactive panels

Each of the eight teaching modules carries a hands-on panel — a sandbox, a slider, or a toggle. They are the point of the module, not decoration. Change the inputs and watch what the change does to the conclusion. The glossary and the references have no panel; they are reference material.

Scope and accuracy

Concepts here are deliberately durable. The telecom examples describe a generic operator and are illustrative, not a specification of any real network. Where a standard is named, treat it as a pointer for further reading and confirm against the current published document.
