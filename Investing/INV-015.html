Crypto Assets

Bitcoin went from a white paper in 2008 to exchange-traded funds on the same shelf as index funds. It has also fallen by three-quarters more than once, and crypto has been the setting for some of the largest frauds in history. This module explains what crypto assets are, how to hold them, how they are taxed and how to size a position you can live with.

The question this module answersIf I own crypto at all, how do I hold it safely, pay the right tax, and keep it small enough that a crash cannot hurt my plan?

Start here

A spreadsheet that everyone keeps a copy of

When you pay with a debit card, your bank updates its private record of who owns what. A crypto asset replaces the bank's record with a public one: a ledger copied across thousands of computers, updated by rules everyone can check, with no company in charge.

That design is genuinely new, and it has attracted serious investment: US regulators now allow exchange-traded products that hold bitcoin. But the price of the asset recorded on that ledger has been one of the most volatile in modern markets. Figure 15.1 shows bitcoin's month-end price since 2015 on a log scale, where equal vertical distances are equal percentage moves.

⚠️

Past returns this large are not a forecast. An asset that rose 70% a year from a tiny base cannot keep doing so as it grows, and many other crypto assets that were popular at the same time have lost nearly everything. The rest of this module is about the risks you control: custody, fraud, taxes and position size.

What this module covers

  1. What a blockchain and bitcoin are, and what stablecoins are.
  2. How volatile crypto has been, measured with real data.
  3. Custody: exchanges, self-custody and exchange-traded funds (ETFs), and why the Securities Investor Protection Corporation (SIPC) and the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC) do not cover crypto.
  4. Spot bitcoin exchange-traded products (ETPs) and how the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) taxes crypto, including the new Form 1099-DA.
  5. How to size a position, and the frauds that cost investors most.

How it works

Blocks, keys and a fixed supply

Bitcoin was described in a nine-page paper, "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System," published in 2008 under the name Satoshi Nakamoto. The problem it solved was how strangers could send digital money without a trusted third party, such as a bank, to stop the same coin being spent twice.

Blockchain
What it is
A shared record of transactions grouped into blocks, each linked to the one before, copied across many computers. In bitcoin, adding a block requires "proof of work," a costly computation, so rewriting history would mean redoing that work faster than everyone else combined.
Used for
Recording who owns which coins and every transfer, without a central bookkeeper.
What it means for you
The ledger itself has proved hard to tamper with. The businesses around it, such as exchanges, lenders and wallet apps, are where most investors have actually lost money.
Private key
What it is
A secret number that authorizes spending from a crypto address. Wallet software usually shows it as a recovery (seed) phrase of 12 or 24 words.
Used for
Signing transactions. The network accepts any correctly signed transfer; it does not know or care who signed it.
What it means for you
Whoever has the key has the coins. There is no password reset, no fraud department and no way to reverse a transfer.

Bitcoin's supply is fixed by its rules. Bitcoin.org explains that new coins are created at a decreasing rate, halving over time, until issuance stops with a total of 21 million bitcoins; each bitcoin divides into 100,000,000 units called satoshis. Supporters see this scarcity as the basis of bitcoin's value, like gold's. A fixed supply does not guarantee a rising price: demand can fall, as every crash in Figure 15.1 shows.

Practitioner depthWhat proof of work does

The white paper describes a chain of blocks secured by hash-based proof of work, "forming a record that cannot be changed without redoing the proof-of-work." Honest participants extend the longest chain, and the longest chain serves as proof of the sequence of events. An attacker would need to outpace everyone else's computing power to rewrite recent history. The cost is energy: the security comes from making each block expensive to produce.

Stablecoinand the GENIUS Act
What it is
A crypto asset designed to hold a fixed value, usually $1. The GENIUS Act, signed on July 18, 2025 (Public Law 119-27), defines a payment stablecoin as one redeemable at a fixed amount, requires US issuers to be approved by a state or federal regulator and to hold at least $1 of permitted reserves for every $1 issued.
Used for
Moving dollars on blockchains, and trading between crypto assets.
What it means for you
The Congressional Research Service summarizes the law as clarifying that payment stablecoins are not securities or commodities and are not federally insured. A stablecoin is not a bank deposit.
Going furtherBitcoin, ether and everything else

Thousands of other crypto assets exist. Ether is the native asset of Ethereum, a blockchain that runs programs ("smart contracts"). Many others are "tokens" issued by projects, and some are openly jokes ("memecoins"). The case for bitcoin as digital scarcity does not transfer to them; each has its own supply rules, insiders and risks. The collapse of TerraUSD, a stablecoin that was not backed by reserves, is in the Case studies tab.

Measured, not described

How much crypto prices move

"Volatile" is an understatement. Using bitcoin's month-end prices from 2015 to 2026, its monthly swings annualize to a volatility of about 66%. For comparison, US stocks' calendar-year volatility since 1928 is about 19% (INV-003). In calendar years, bitcoin's returns have ranged from −73% to +1,324%.

Drawdownand the arithmetic of recovery
What it is
The fall from a previous peak. A 75% drawdown leaves 25 cents of every dollar.
Used for
Measuring how painful an asset has been to hold, not just how volatile.
What it means for you
After a 75% fall the price must rise 300% just to get back to even. Most people who buy near a peak and panic near the bottom never see the recovery.
Gain needed to recover = 1 ÷ (1 − loss) − 1    e.g. 1 ÷ 0.25 − 1 = 300%
Going furtherWhy the starting date changes everything

Someone who bought at the end of December 2017 at about $13,900 was down 75% by January 2019 and did not see a higher month-end price until November 2020. Someone who bought at the October 2021 month-end, $61,300, waited until March 2024. Averages over the whole history hide how different individual investors' experiences were, depending on when they bought and whether they held on.

Practitioner depthHow the volatility figure is computed

Monthly log returns ln(Pt ÷ Pt−1), computed from the month-end prices of January 2015 through September 2026 (140 monthly returns), have a standard deviation of about 19% a month; multiplied by √12, that is about 66% a year. The most recent 60 months give about 55%. Month-end data understate the intramonth range, so true peak-to-trough falls were somewhat larger than the month-end figures quoted here.

Who holds the keys

Exchanges, self-custody and funds

Owning crypto means controlling private keys, directly or through someone else. There are three main arrangements, each with a different way to lose money.

What is not protected

Two protections that ordinary investors rely on do not extend to crypto.

  • SIPC. The Securities Investor Protection Corporation protects securities and cash at a failed brokerage firm. SIPC states that it does not protect any digital or crypto asset that does not qualify as a "security" under the Securities Investor Protection Act, and that digital asset securities that are unregistered investment contracts are not protected even if held at a SIPC-member firm. SIPC never protects against a fall in price.
  • FDIC. The Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation insures deposits at FDIC-insured banks. Its list of products it does not insure includes stocks, bonds, mutual funds, annuities and crypto assets.

Shares of a registered spot bitcoin exchange-traded product held at a SIPC-member brokerage are securities; if the broker failed, SIPC's protection would apply to the missing shares, not to any loss in bitcoin's price.

Self-custody
What it is
Holding your own private keys, typically in a hardware wallet kept offline, with the recovery phrase written down and stored securely.
Used for
Removing the risk that an exchange or lender fails or freezes withdrawals.
What it means for you
You become your own bank, security department and estate planner. Your executor needs to know the assets exist and how to reach them (INV-084).
Going furtherCrypto "yield" and lending platforms

A yield on crypto comes from lending it to someone or putting it at risk; it is not a bank deposit rate, and the FDIC does not insure crypto assets. If a platform pays much more than Treasury bills, ask where the return comes from, who holds your coins in the meantime and who bears the loss if the borrower fails.

Practitioner depthSIPC's test for digital assets

SIPC explains that some digital assets may be securities if they are investment contracts, but that an investment contract, digital asset or otherwise, must be registered with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) to be a "security" under the Securities Investor Protection Act. Unregistered digital asset securities are therefore not protected, even at a SIPC-member firm, and assets that are not securities at all are never protected.

The regulated wrapper and the IRS

Spot bitcoin ETPs, and crypto as property

On January 10, 2024, the SEC approved the listing and trading of a number of spot bitcoin exchange-traded product (ETP) shares: funds that hold bitcoin itself through a custodian and trade on stock exchanges. The SEC chair's statement that day was careful: "While we approved the listing and trading of certain spot bitcoin ETP shares today, we did not approve or endorse bitcoin," adding that investors "should remain cautious about the myriad risks associated with bitcoin." On September 17, 2025 the SEC approved generic listing standards that let exchanges list qualifying commodity-based trust shares, including those holding digital assets, without a separate SEC rule filing for each.

Spot bitcoin ETP
What it is
An exchange-traded product that owns bitcoin, not futures. Its shares track the bitcoin price, less the fund's expense ratio.
Used for
Holding bitcoin exposure in an ordinary brokerage account or individual retirement account (IRA), with ordinary tax forms and no keys to manage.
What it means for you
Convenience, not safety from price swings. Compare expense ratios; they differ across funds that hold the same asset.

How the IRS taxes crypto

Since IRS Notice 2014-21, virtual currency has been treated as property for federal tax purposes. The IRS's digital assets page states that digital assets are considered property, not currency. That single rule drives everything else:

What you doTax result
Buy crypto with dollars and hold itNo tax. Moving it between your own wallets is not a sale either (IRS virtual currency FAQ 38).
Sell it for dollarsCapital gain or loss: price received minus your cost basis.
Trade one crypto asset for anotherA sale of the first asset, with gain or loss.
Spend it on goods or servicesAlso a sale: gain or loss on the crypto spent.
Receive it as pay or a rewardOrdinary income at its dollar value when received; that value becomes your basis.

Gains are short-term if you held the asset as a capital asset for one year or less and long-term if more than one year, the same holding periods as stocks (INV-030). Every federal return asks, yes or no, whether you received or disposed of a digital asset during the year.

Form 1099-DAnew broker reporting
What it is
The form brokers use to report digital asset sales to you and the IRS. Under Treasury and IRS final regulations, brokers must report gross proceeds for transactions on or after January 1, 2025, and basis for certain transactions on or after January 1, 2026.
Used for
Matching taxpayers' reported gains with brokers' records, as with Form 1099-B for stocks.
What it means for you
The IRS now sees your sales. Keep your own records of what you paid, especially for assets bought before 2026 or moved between platforms, because the form may show proceeds without basis.
Going furtherRecords to keep

For each acquisition: date, amount, dollar cost and fees, platform or wallet. For each disposal: date, amount, dollar value received. Transfers between your own wallets should be documented so they are not mistaken for sales. A spot ETP in a brokerage account avoids most of this, because the broker tracks basis like any other security.

Practitioner depthHolding crypto funds in retirement accounts

Inside an IRA or 401(k), gains on a spot ETP are not taxed each year; a traditional account taxes withdrawals as ordinary income and a Roth account can make qualified withdrawals tax-free (INV-032). A very volatile asset in a Roth concentrates both the tax-free upside and the risk of a permanent loss of scarce Roth space.

How much, if any

Size it by the loss you can survive

A position's share of your statement understates its share of your risk. Because crypto is several times as volatile as a diversified portfolio, a small allocation supplies a large share of the ups and downs. The calculator shows both: the dollars you would lose in a crash, and crypto's share of total portfolio risk.

Risk contribution
What it is
The share of a portfolio's total ups and downs that comes from one holding. It depends on the holding's weight, its volatility and its correlation with everything else.
Used for
Sizing volatile positions by their effect on the whole portfolio rather than by dollars alone.
What it means for you
A position's dollar weight understates its risk weight whenever it is much more volatile than the rest of your holdings.

With the defaults (5% in crypto, crypto volatility 60%, the rest 12%, correlation 0.3), crypto supplies about 12% of the portfolio's risk. At 10% it supplies about 29%. A 75% crypto crash in a year when everything else falls 10% costs a 5% position $3,750 of $100,000 and the whole portfolio about 13%, compared with 10% without the crypto.

💡

A practical rule. Hold crypto, if at all, only after the emergency fund, the full employer match and high-interest debt are handled, and only in an amount whose total loss would not change your retirement date or your children's education. For most households that means a low single-digit percentage or nothing. This is a course guideline, not a law or an industry standard.

Going furtherRebalancing a very volatile asset

If you decide on a target, such as 2%, rebalancing back to it trims the position after surges and adds after crashes. In a year like 2017 (+1,324%), an unrebalanced 2% position would have become roughly a fifth of the portfolio. Setting the target in writing, in an investment policy statement (INV-028), is what keeps a small bet small.

Practitioner depthThe formula behind the calculator

For two parts with weights w and 1 − w, volatilities σc and σr and correlation ρ, portfolio variance is σp² = w²σc² + (1 − w)²σr² + 2w(1 − w)ρσcσr. The crypto part contributes w²σc² + w(1 − w)ρσcσr. At w = 5%, σc = 60%, σr = 12%, ρ = 0.3: portfolio variance is 0.0159 (volatility 12.6%) and crypto contributes 0.0019, about 12%.

Real history

Where crypto investors actually lost money

Case study 1FTX, 2022
What happened
FTX was an international cryptocurrency exchange founded by Samuel Bankman-Fried. Federal prosecutors showed that from 2019 to 2022 he misappropriated billions of dollars of customer deposits, channeling them to his trading firm, Alameda Research, while telling customers their deposits were kept safe and separate. He was convicted and, on March 28, 2024, sentenced to 25 years in prison and over $11 billion in forfeiture. The US Attorney described the fraud as "stealing over $8 billion of his customers' money."
What it teaches
Crypto held on an exchange is only as safe as the exchange. The blockchain worked perfectly; the company holding the keys did not.
Source
U.S. Attorney's Office, Southern District of New York, "Samuel Bankman-Fried Sentenced To 25 Years In Prison" (March 28, 2024).
Case study 2TerraUSD, May 2022
What happened
TerraUSD (UST) was a crypto asset marketed as a stablecoin worth $1. When it lost its peg in May 2022, the SEC says, the collapse "wiped out $40 billion in market value nearly overnight." On April 5, 2024 a jury found Terraform Labs and its founder Do Kwon liable for securities fraud, and in June 2024 they agreed to pay more than $4.5 billion.
What it teaches
"Stable" is a claim, not a property. Ask what backs a stablecoin and whether you can redeem it for dollars. The 2025 GENIUS Act now requires US payment stablecoin issuers to hold one-for-one reserves.
Source
SEC press release 2024-73, "Terraform and Kwon to Pay $4.5 Billion Following Fraud Verdict" (June 13, 2024).
Case study 3Crypto scams reported to the FBI, 2025
What happened
The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) received 181,565 complaints involving cryptocurrency in 2025, with reported losses of $11.366 billion, up 22% from 2024. Crypto investment fraud alone accounted for 61,559 complaints and $7.228 billion. Across all internet crime, reported losses passed $20 billion.
What it teaches
The largest crypto losses for individuals come from fraud: fake investment platforms, romance and "investment club" schemes, impersonators. Anyone who asks you to move money into crypto to "protect" it or to join a platform they control is running a scam (INV-062).
Source
Federal Bureau of Investigation, 2025 IC3 Annual Report.

Decisions

Four households, four crypto questions

Scenario 1 · Jordan

Jordan's friends made money in bitcoin last year. She has $3,000 saved and $28,000 of student loans at 6.8%. She wants to put $2,000 into bitcoin on an app. What should she do?

Buy nowHer capacity to take risk is higher than a retiree's, but her cushion is $3,000. A 75% fall, which has happened twice, would take $1,500 and leave her one car repair from new debt.
Base first, then a small sliceRight. The match is an immediate 50% return and each dollar of 6.8% debt repaid earns a certain 6.8%.
Borrow to buyLeverage on the most volatile asset she could own. Card interest is certain; the gain is not.
The principle: speculate only with money whose loss would not change your plans.
Scenario 2 · Monica

Worried that $400,000 will not carry her through retirement and still leave something for Eli, Monica, 58, considers moving her $40,000 taxable account into bitcoin to "catch up." What is the better move?

Move itBitcoin fell 64% in 2022. A fall like that would leave about $14,400 of her $40,000, with nine years to go before retirement and no second income to rebuild it.
Keep it diversified and close the gap other waysRight. For 2026 she can add an $8,000 catch-up contribution to her 401(k), rising to $11,250 at ages 60 to 63, and each year of work to 67 adds savings and shortens the retirement her savings must cover. A special needs trust and an ABLE account protect what she leaves Eli (INV-084).
The principle: the less time you have to recover, the less volatility you can afford; the reliable way to catch up is to save more, not to bet more.
Scenario 3 · Ruth

Ruth has been messaging with a friendly stranger online who shows her screenshots of large gains on a "crypto investment platform" and offers to help her open an account and move $50,000 from her savings. What should she do?

Test with a littleThese schemes often let a victim withdraw a small "profit" to build trust before asking for much more. Money sent in crypto is very hard to recover.
Stop and reportRight. This is the pattern of crypto investment fraud, the largest category of crypto losses reported to the FBI in 2025 ($7.2 billion).
The principle: an unsolicited "friend" plus a platform you cannot verify plus urgency equals fraud.
Scenario 4 · The Harpers

The Harpers want 2% of their $1.1 million, about $22,000, in bitcoin. Where and how?

Offshore yield platformAdds counterparty and fraud risk to price risk, with no SIPC protection. A 10% yield on crypto means someone is taking risk with their coins.
ETP, target, rebalanceA defensible way to hold a small position. Inside an IRA, gains are not taxed each year; in taxable accounts, basis is tracked like any security.
The principle: if you own it, own it simply, and keep it small on purpose.

Practice

Work the numbers yourself

Exercise 1

You bought 0.5 bitcoin for $20,000. Two years later you spend 0.1 bitcoin on a purchase when bitcoin is worth $100,000. What is your taxable gain on the 0.1 bitcoin, in dollars?

Basis per bitcoin: $20,000 ÷ 0.5 = $40,000, so 0.1 bitcoin cost $4,000. Value spent: 0.1 × $100,000 = $10,000. Gain = $6,000, long-term because held more than a year.
Exercise 2

An asset falls 75%. What percentage gain does it need to return to its previous value?

$1 becomes $0.25. To return to $1: 1 ÷ 0.25 = 4 times, a gain of 300%.
Exercise 3

A $200,000 portfolio holds 3% in crypto. Crypto falls 80%. How many dollars does the crypto position lose?

Position: $200,000 × 0.03 = $6,000. Loss: $6,000 × 0.80 = $4,800, 2.4% of the portfolio.
Exercise 4

A portfolio holds 5% in crypto and 95% in a diversified mix. In a bad year crypto falls 75% and the mix falls 10%. What is the whole portfolio's return, in percent?

0.05 × (−75) = −3.75; 0.95 × (−10) = −9.5. Total −13.25%, against −10% without the crypto.
