Annuities and Insurance Products

Insurance companies sell investments too: annuities that promise income or protection, and life insurance policies that build cash value. Some of these products solve problems nothing else can, above all the risk of outliving your money. Others bundle protection you may not need with fees you cannot see. This module shows how to tell the difference.

The question this module answersWhen is an annuity or a cash-value policy the right tool, and what should I check before signing?

Start here

Life insurance in reverse

Life insurance pays your family if you die too soon. An annuity can do the opposite: it pays you for as long as you live, however long that turns out to be. Both work by pooling many people's money and many people's lifetimes.

The question an annuity answers is one no stock or bond fund can: how long will you need the money? Nobody knows their own lifespan, and the range is wide. Figure 17.1 uses the Social Security Administration's (SSA's) latest period life table. Of 100 women aged 68, like Ruth, about 89 are expected to reach 75, 58 to reach 85, 36 to reach 90 and 15 to reach 95.

That is the ordinary, legitimate case for annuities. The problem is that the word covers very different products, from a simple contract that turns $100,000 into a monthly check, to complex contracts with a dozen moving parts, multi-year surrender charges and generous sales commissions. Life insurance is similar: term insurance is simple protection; cash-value policies combine protection with an investment account and higher premiums.

One word, four products

Fixed, indexed, variable and income annuities

The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) describes an annuity as a contract between you and an insurance company with two phases: an accumulation phase, when you pay in and the money grows, and a payout phase, when the insurer pays you, either as a stream of payments or a lump sum. The products differ in how the money grows and who bears the investment risk.

Income annuitysingle premium immediate (SPIA) or deferred (DIA)
What it is
You pay a lump sum; the insurer pays a fixed amount for life, starting now (a single premium immediate annuity) or at a later age (a deferred income annuity). Options add payments to a surviving spouse or a refund to heirs, at the cost of lower income.
Used for
Turning part of your savings into a pension-like paycheck that lasts as long as you do.
What it means for you
The simplest annuity and often the cheapest per dollar of guaranteed income. The trade-off is irreversibility: the lump sum is gone.
Fixed indexed annuity
What it is
An annuity whose credited return depends on a market index, within limits. The SEC notes that indexed annuities that are not securities are regulated by the states and include minimum guarantees that limit, and in many cases eliminate, the potential for investment losses.
Used for
Seeking some market-linked growth without losing principal to market declines.
What it means for you
You give up much of the upside, usually including all dividends, and accept a long surrender period. It is not "the market without the risk."
Variable annuity
What it is
A tax-deferred account invested in mutual-fund-like subaccounts, wrapped in an insurance contract with a death benefit and optional living benefits. It is a security registered with the SEC.
Used for
Tax deferral beyond retirement-account limits, and guarantees such as a minimum death benefit or a guaranteed minimum income.
What it means for you
Layers of fees: the SEC cites a mortality and expense risk charge typically about 1.25% a year and administrative fees about 0.15%, plus the subaccounts' expenses and any riders.
Going furtherWho regulates which annuity

The SEC regulates only indexed annuities that are securities; those carry a prospectus and can expose investors to losses. All indexed annuities are regulated by state insurance commissions, and the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (FINRA) regulates the securities industry, including sellers of some indexed annuities. Variable annuities are securities. Fixed and income annuities are insurance products regulated by the states. Complaints go to the matching regulator: your state insurance department or, for securities, FINRA and the SEC.

Practitioner depthThe two phases, and what "annuitize" means

During the accumulation phase you pay in and the value grows. During the payout phase the insurer pays you, either periodic payments or a lump sum. Turning the account into lifetime payments is called annuitizing; it gives up access to the lump sum in exchange for the income guarantee.

Insurance against a long life

Mortality credits: why an annuity can pay more

Suppose 1,000 women aged 68 each put $100,000 in a pool invested in bonds, and agree the pool will pay equal yearly checks to whoever is alive. Those who die early leave their money in the pool, so it can pay each survivor more than she could safely withdraw alone. That extra is called a mortality credit, and it grows with age. An insurance company runs such a pool, charges for its costs and profit, and guarantees the payments.

Mortality credits
What it is
The extra income a lifetime annuity pool can pay because the premiums of members who die early stay in the pool for those who live longer.
Used for
Explaining why a lifetime annuity can pay more than a bond portfolio could safely pay one person.
What it means for you
The credit is small at 60 and large at 85. It is the one return only an annuity pool can offer, and its price is giving up the lump sum.
Yearly income = premium × (1 − costs) ÷ Σt [ chance of being alive at year t ÷ (1 + interest rate)t ]

With the defaults, a 68-year-old woman paying $100,000 would receive about $8,350 a year in this model, a payout rate of 8.35%, and her cumulative payments would pass her premium at age 80, which she has about a 76% chance of reaching. Actual quotes are usually lower, because insurers price with mortality tables for annuity buyers, who live longer than the general population, and because of their costs and interest-rate assumptions. Get several real quotes before relying on any number.

💡

Social Security is already an inflation-adjusted income annuity. Ruth's $2,900 a month is lifetime income that rises with prices. For many retirees, delaying Social Security is one way to buy more inflation-adjusted lifetime income (INV-078). A private income annuity makes most sense after that, to cover essential spending that Social Security and any pension do not.

Going furtherDeferred income annuities and QLACs

Because payments start later and fewer buyers survive to collect, a deferred income annuity buys much more income per dollar. In the estimator, a 65-year-old woman who defers income to 80 receives about $35,400 a year per $100,000. Bought inside an individual retirement account (IRA) as a qualifying longevity annuity contract (QLAC), its value is left out when figuring required minimum distributions until payments begin. The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) premium limit is $210,000 for 2026 (Notice 2025-67).

Practitioner depthHow annuity payments are taxed

For an annuity bought with after-tax money, IRS Publication 939's General Rule treats part of each payment as a tax-free return of your investment, based on the ratio of your investment in the contract to the total expected return; the rest is ordinary income. Annuities inside an IRA are taxed like any IRA withdrawal: fully as ordinary income if the contributions were pre-tax.

How the limits and fees work

Reading an indexed or variable annuity

The SEC's bulletin on indexed annuities lists the features that reduce your share of an index's gain. Each is explained with the SEC's own example.

Crediting method
What it is
The contract's rule for turning an index's change into the interest credited to an indexed annuity: which index, over what period (point-to-point or averaging), and with which limits.
Used for
Comparing indexed annuities, whose crediting methods can differ more than their headline rates.
What it means for you
Two contracts on the same index can credit very different returns. Ask for the crediting method in writing and how often the insurer can change the cap or rate.

Variable annuity fees and taxes

The SEC's guide lists the charges: the mortality and expense risk charge (typically about 1.25% a year), administrative fees (typically about 0.15% a year, or a flat fee such as $25 or $30), the underlying funds' expenses, and fees for optional features such as guaranteed minimum income benefits. Earnings grow tax-deferred, but withdrawals of earnings are taxed at ordinary income rates rather than lower capital-gains rates, and withdrawals before age 59½ may face a 10% federal tax penalty.

⚠️

No extra tax benefit inside an IRA. The SEC is direct: if you are investing in a variable annuity through a tax-advantaged retirement plan such as a 401(k) or IRA, "you will get no additional tax advantage from the variable annuity." You would be paying for tax deferral you already have.

Going furtherWithdrawals come from earnings first

For an annuity bought with after-tax money, IRS Publication 575 explains that a withdrawal before annuity payments begin is allocated first to earnings (taxable) and then to your investment (tax-free). In the IRS example, a $7,000 withdrawal from a contract worth $16,000 with a $10,000 investment is $6,000 taxable and $1,000 tax-free.

Practitioner depthDeath benefits and living benefits

The standard variable annuity death benefit pays the greater of the account value or a guaranteed minimum, such as all purchase payments minus withdrawals (SEC). A guaranteed minimum income benefit guarantees a minimum level of annuity payments even if the account has fallen. These guarantees are real and can be valuable in a crash, but each optional benefit carries its own yearly fee, and the contract's fine print determines what the guarantee is actually worth.

The cost of changing your mind

Surrender periods, exchanges and warnings

Surrender charge
What it is
A fee for withdrawing money, beyond any free amount, during the first years of an annuity. The SEC says the surrender period is typically six to eight years for variable annuities, sometimes as long as ten, and gives an example of 7% in the first year, 6% in the second, 5% in the third, and so on. Its bulletin on indexed annuities says their surrender periods typically last six to ten years, or even longer.
Used for
Recovering the sales commission and other costs the insurer paid up front.
What it means for you
Money in a new annuity is not liquid for years. A surrender charge on top of a market loss can turn a bad year into a very bad one.
1035 exchange
What it is
Section 1035 of the tax code lets you exchange one annuity (or life insurance policy) for another without paying tax on the gains.
Used for
Leaving an expensive old contract for a better one.
What it means for you
The SEC warns that you may pay surrender charges on the old annuity if it is still in its surrender period, that a new surrender charge period generally begins with the new annuity, and that bonus credits can carry higher expenses that outweigh them. Ask who benefits from the exchange.

Rules that protect buyers

  • Free look. The SEC notes many contracts give a free-look period of ten or more days to cancel without surrender charges.
  • Best interest. The National Association of Insurance Commissioners' (NAIC's) Suitability in Annuity Transactions Model Regulation, revised in 2020, requires insurance producers to act in the consumer's best interest when recommending an annuity; states adopt it individually.
  • Securities rules. Variable annuities are securities, so brokers selling them are subject to SEC and FINRA oversight, including Regulation Best Interest.
Going furtherQuestions to ask before signing
  1. What is every annual fee, as a percentage and in dollars?
  2. What is the surrender schedule, year by year, and how much can I withdraw free each year?
  3. What exactly is guaranteed, by whom, and what do the guarantees cost?
  4. How is the seller paid, and how much?
  5. Could a simpler, cheaper product, such as an income annuity, Treasury bonds or an index fund, meet the same need?
Practitioner depthBonus annuities

Some variable annuities add a bonus to your contract value, typically 1% to 5% of purchase payments, according to the SEC. The SEC cautions that such contracts may carry higher expenses that can outweigh the bonus, and a bonus offered to encourage an exchange from an existing contract may be offset by a new surrender period.

Protection, or investment?

Cash-value life insurance versus term plus investing

The NAIC explains that life insurance policies fall into two classes: term and cash value. Term insurance covers a set period and is intended to provide lower-cost coverage. Whole life, universal life and variable life are types of cash-value insurance, which last for life and build a cash value, with higher premiums. The NAIC also warns that in some policies "the values are low in the early" years and build later.

Buy term and invest the difference
What it is
Buying term insurance for the years your family depends on your income, and investing the premium you save compared with a cash-value policy.
Used for
Separating insurance from investing so each can be done at low cost.
What it means for you
It works only if you actually invest the difference every year. The comparison hinges on the policy's true net rate of return, which the calculator lets you vary.

With the illustrative defaults, a $6,000 cash-value premium versus a $450 term premium over 30 years: if the policy's cash value earns a net 2.5% a year on premiums, it reaches about $270,000; investing the $5,550 difference at 6% less a 0.5% tax drag reaches about $424,000. The policy would need to earn about 5.1% a year, net of all its costs, to break even. The numbers are illustrations, not quotes; ask any insurer for an in-force illustration showing guaranteed and non-guaranteed cash values by year, and run them here.

Tax features of life insurance

Cash-value policies do have real tax advantages. Death benefits paid because the insured dies are generally excluded from income (Internal Revenue Code section 101(a)). Cash value grows without yearly tax. But a policy funded faster than the "7-pay test" in section 7702A becomes a modified endowment contract: loans and withdrawals are then taxed as income first, and taxable amounts can face a 10% additional tax (section 72(v)). For most households whose need is to protect dependents, these features matter less than the cost of coverage.

If an insurer fails: state guaranty associations

Insurance is regulated by the states. When an insurer is liquidated, state guaranty associations, funded by assessments on other insurers in the state, continue coverage up to limits set by state law. The National Organization of Life & Health Insurance Guaranty Associations (NOLHGA) reports that most states cover annuities up to $250,000 in present value of annuity benefits, life insurance death benefits up to $300,000 and cash surrender values up to $100,000, with limits that vary by state (as of June 1, 2025).

Going furtherHow much coverage?

The NAIC suggests starting from how much your family depends on you financially, the value of the services you provide, and end-of-life expenses. A common approach is to add the income your dependents would need for the years until they are independent, plus debts and planned costs such as college, then subtract savings and existing coverage. INV-023 works through it for each household.

Practitioner depthReading an in-force illustration

An illustration projects a policy's premiums, cash values and death benefits year by year, usually in two columns: guaranteed values and non-guaranteed values based on the insurer's current crediting rates and charges. The NAIC advises asking your advisor to explain future values and benefits. To use the calculator above, find the year-20 or year-30 cash value in each column and solve for the rate that turns your premiums into that value.

Real history

When an insurer fails

Case study 1Colorado Bankers Life, liquidated in 2024
What happened
On November 30, 2024, Colorado Bankers Life Insurance Company was placed in liquidation by order of the Wake County Superior Court in North Carolina. State life and health insurance guaranty associations began providing coverage for its policies under their laws, subject to exclusions and limits. On January 1, 2026, the guaranty associations entered an agreement under which Continental General Insurance Company assumed responsibility for the covered obligations.
What it teaches
Insurer failures still happen, and the guaranty system is designed for them. Coverage follows the policyholder's state of residence and its limits, so amounts above the limit become claims against the failed insurer's remaining assets.
Source
NOLHGA, "Important Information for Policyholders of Colorado Bankers Life Insurance Company and Bankers Life Insurance Company."
Case study 2Executive Life, 1991 and 2013
What happened
NOLHGA's list of insolvent insurers records the 1991 liquidation of Executive Life Insurance Company in California and the 2013 liquidation of its New York affiliate, Executive Life Insurance Company of New York. NOLHGA reports that in the Executive Life of New York and Penn Treaty liquidations, guaranty associations paid out billions of dollars in benefits to affected annuity holders and policyholders, and that the Executive Life annuities were later moved to a financially sound insurer.
What it teaches
An annuity promise can last 30 years; the insurer's strength matters for all of them. Check an insurer's financial strength before buying, and spread large annuity purchases across insurers so each stays within your state's limit.
Source
NOLHGA, "Insolvent Insurance Companies" and "Policyholder Protection in Action."

Decisions

Four households, four insurance questions

Scenario 1 · Ruth

Ruth wants her essential bills covered for life. Her Social Security is $2,900 a month. She is offered a variable annuity with a living-benefit rider for $300,000 of her IRA, and separately considers a simple income annuity for $150,000. Which fits her need better?

Variable annuityInside an IRA it adds no tax benefit (SEC). The rider and contract fees compound for decades, and a new surrender period locks the money up just as required distributions begin in 2031.
Plain income annuity for part of the gapA sound approach to compare. It directly insures longevity, has no surrender schedule to manage, and leaves most of her IRA invested and liquid. Her daughter Anna should see the quotes too.
Refuse all annuitiesSimple income annuities can be one of the few ways to guarantee income for life; ruling them out means self-insuring against living to 95, which a 68-year-old woman has about a 15% chance of doing.
The principle: buy the simplest product that insures the specific risk you have.
Scenario 2 · The Riveras

With Sofia (7) and Leo (4), the Riveras need life insurance. An agent recommends whole life policies with premiums of about $500 a month; comparable term coverage costs a fraction of that. What should they do?

Whole lifeAt that premium they may be underinsured to afford it, and the early cash values are low. The calculator shows the policy must earn a high net rate to beat investing the difference.
Term plus their other goalsRight. The NAIC describes term as the lower-cost coverage. Their $41,000 of idle cash and missing 529 plan are better uses of the difference (INV-023, INV-034).
The principle: insure the income your family depends on, at the lowest cost, and invest separately.
Scenario 3 · The Harpers

An advisor proposes that the Harpers fund an indexed universal life policy heavily, as a "tax-free retirement account." They already have $1.1 million saved and will retire in five or six years. What should they check?

Tax-free is enoughLoans from an overfunded policy can become taxable if it is a modified endowment contract, and caps limit growth. Tax treatment does not make a costly product a good one.
Check need, fees and alternativesRight. Roth conversions in low-income early-retirement years may achieve tax-free growth more cheaply and transparently (INV-038).
The principle: a tax wrapper is worth only what is left after its costs.
Scenario 4 · Monica

Monica's employer provides group life insurance equal to one year's salary, $92,000. Her son Eli, 13, has special needs and will likely need support for life. Is that enough?

One year's salaryFor a single parent whose child may never be financially independent, $92,000 is a short bridge, and group coverage usually ends when the job does, as hers will when she retires at about 67.
Add individual term, payable to a trustRight. A 20-year policy bought at 58 runs to 78 and stays in force if she changes jobs or retires, and the NAIC describes term as the lower-cost form of coverage (INV-023). Naming a supplemental needs trust as beneficiary keeps the money from becoming Eli's own assets, which could cost him SSI and Medicaid (INV-084, INV-085).
The principle: size life insurance by who depends on you and for how long, and name a beneficiary who can receive it without harm.

Practice

Work the numbers yourself

Exercise 1

From IRS Publication 575: before annuity payments begin, you withdraw $7,000 from an annuity bought with after-tax money. The contract's cash value is $16,000 and your investment in it is $10,000. How many dollars of the withdrawal are taxable?

Withdrawals come from earnings first. Earnings = $16,000 − $10,000 = $6,000, so $6,000 is taxable and $1,000 is a tax-free return of investment. Before 59½, the taxable part may also face the 10% additional tax.
Exercise 2

An indexed annuity has an 80% participation rate and an 8% cap. The index's price rises 12%. What return is credited, in percent?

80% × 12% = 9.6%, above the cap, so the credit is 8%. With dividends excluded, the index's total return was higher still.
Exercise 3

Using the SEC's example schedule (7% in year 1, 6% in year 2, 5% in year 3), how many dollars of surrender charge apply if you withdraw $100,000 in year 3? Ignore any free-withdrawal amount.

Year 3 charge 5%: $100,000 × 0.05 = $5,000. Most contracts allow some free withdrawal each year, often a percentage of the value; check yours.
Exercise 4

A Pennsylvania resident holds a single $400,000 annuity (present value of benefits) with an insurer that is liquidated. How many dollars are above the state guaranty association's annuity limit?

Pennsylvania's annuity limit is $250,000 (NOLHGA). $400,000 − $250,000 = $150,000, which becomes a claim against the insurer's estate.
